# ADR 0006: World ages and society advancement

Status: Accepted  
Date: 2026-08-14

## Context

The campaign is intended to run from tribal society around 10,000 BCE into a speculative future. It needs recognizable historical chapters without implying that every society develops at the same time or in the same way. A fixed date-only transition would make research irrelevant, while a pure technology race would discard the historical timeline and encourage narrow beelines.

The game also needs to distinguish changes to the global historical environment from the local capabilities of an individual tribe, state, or civilization.

## Decision

### Two related progression states

- **World Age** is the shared global historical environment. It controls which global systems, pressures, discoveries, and interactions can exist.
- **Society Stage** is the actual institutional and technological development of one polity. Societies advance independently and may remain behind the current World Age.

Changing the World Age never grants every society the new age's technologies, institutions, buildings, units, or identity.

### Two ways for an age to begin

Every age has a soft historical date at which the next World Age emerges naturally. This date opens the next age to eligible societies; it does not upgrade them automatically.

Every age also culminates in a synthesis technology. The synthesis requires broad progress across several knowledge branches and relevant lived accomplishments. It must not be reachable by following one narrow technology path.

Researching the synthesis before the next age has emerged naturally unlocks an authoritative **Usher in the Next Age** decision. A society may use that decision to begin the next World Age early. The decision is no longer available after another society ushers in the age or the natural unlock occurs.

### Effects of ushering in an age

- The next World Age and its global rules become active.
- The pioneering society receives bounded prestige and influence, plus a limited choice that helps characterize the emerging age.
- Other societies remain at their existing Society Stage.
- Earlier technologies remain available. Contact, trade, observation, migration, diplomacy, espionage, and conquest can accelerate diffusion and create catch-up opportunities.
- The pioneer does not receive a permanent compounding research bonus.

### Civilization continuity

An age transition does not force a civilization swap. A polity can retain its identity, reform it, adopt a historically and contextually plausible successor, or form a hybrid identity. Civilization evolution is informed by culture, geography, institutions, conquests, alliances, and player decisions rather than by the World Age alone.

### Working World Age timeline

The dates below are reference points for natural emergence, not universal claims about when every society reached a stage.

| World Age | Soft natural timeline |
| --- | ---: |
| Tribal Age | 10,000–6,000 BCE |
| Community Age | 6,000–3,500 BCE |
| First States Age | 3,500–1,200 BCE |
| Imperial Age | 1,200 BCE–500 CE |
| Age of Realms | 500–1,000 |
| Commercial Age | 1,000–1,450 |
| Global Exchange Age | 1,450–1,750 |
| Industrial Age | 1,750–1,870 |
| Mass Society Age | 1,870–1,914 |
| Age of Upheaval | 1,914–1,945 |
| Atomic Age | 1,945–1,991 |
| Information Age | 1,991–2,040 |
| Planetary Age | 2,040 onward |

The Planetary Age is deliberately open-ended. Its mature form should branch according to the campaign into outcomes such as renewal, abundance, fracture, machine intelligence, or technologically enforced control instead of asserting one canonical future.

## Consequences

- Multiplayer needs one deterministic, authoritative World Age transition and independently replicated Society Stages.
- Age capstones must combine research prerequisites with observable simulation accomplishments.
- A leading society can alter history without instantly dragging the entire world forward.
- Lagging societies remain playable because new knowledge can diffuse, but they must still build the institutions and material capacity needed to use it.
- Historical dates provide recognizable pacing while allowing alternate history through early transitions.
- The operational day/night clock and the multi-millennial campaign calendar still require a separate compression decision.
